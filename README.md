# Mecanum platform: FreeCAD model

A parametric FreeCAD model of a small mecanum-wheel robot: the 14 printed parts and the assemblies they make, from
one roller up to the whole robot. Every document is generated by a Python script, and every part is a PartDesign
Body of fully constrained sketches with named dimensions, so it can be changed in FreeCAD like any part drawn by
hand. Values that several parts share live in one spreadsheet.

The wheel is based on the mecanum wheel Evan Vadeboncoeur designed in Onshape:
<https://cad.onshape.com/documents/e0f05c9b0d7d917d65ea2bd9/w/0eb9bfe9671daf3c00b1d8af/e/2667fa84f502811442702435>

## Opening it

FreeCAD 1.1 or newer, with the Fasteners workbench to change the screws. Open `model/assembly/Robot.FCStd` for
the whole robot, or any part in `model/wheel`, `model/drive`, `model/body` or `model/bought`.

To change the design, open `model/Parameters.FCStd`, edit a cell in its spreadsheet and recompute: every part that
uses the value follows. Dimensions that belong to one part only are named constraints in that part's sketches.
`docs/design.md` describes what each part is and how it is built.

## The parts

[BOM.md](BOM.md) is the bill of materials: every printed part, bought part, screw and insert with its quantity,
counted from the built robot.

| Part | Document | In the robot |
|---|---|---|
| Ellipsoidal roller | `model/wheel/Ellipsoidal_Roller.FCStd` | 36 |
| Roller pin | `model/wheel/Pin_For_Ball_Bearing.FCStd` | 36 |
| Washer | `model/wheel/Washer.FCStd` | 72 |
| Wheel side, motor side, left- and right-handed | `model/wheel/Motor_Side_Left.FCStd`, `Motor_Side_Right.FCStd` | 2 + 2 |
| Wheel side, outer side, left- and right-handed | `model/wheel/Outer_Side_Left.FCStd`, `Outer_Side_Right.FCStd` | 2 + 2 |
| Motor hub | `model/drive/Nema_17_Mount.FCStd` | 4 |
| Body quarters | `model/body/Body_Low_Right.FCStd`, `Body_Low_Left`, `Body_Upper_Right`, `Body_Upper_Left` | 1 each |
| Body alignment plate | `model/body/Body_Alignment_Plate.FCStd` | 1 |
| Controller baseplate | `model/body/Baseplate_MotionSystemController.FCStd` | 1 |

The assemblies are in `model/assembly`: `Roller_Assembly`, `Wheel_Left`, `Wheel_Right`, `Body` and `Robot`.

The bought parts are in the assemblies as interface models, drawn from their standard dimensions, so the robot
shows where everything goes:

| Bought part | Document | In the robot |
|---|---|---|
| NEMA 17 stepper motor | `model/bought/NEMA17_Stepper.FCStd` | 4 |
| 2200 mAh battery | `model/bought/Battery_2200mAh.FCStd` | 1 |
| 673ZZ ball bearing, 3 × 6 × 2.5 | `model/bought/Ball_Bearing_673ZZ.FCStd` | 72 |
| M3 × 5 hex spacer under the controller board | `model/bought/Spacer_M3x5.FCStd` | 4 |
| Screws, ISO 4762 and ISO 10642, M3 × 4 to 10 | in the assemblies that use them | 80 |
| M3 heat-set inserts, 3, 4 and 5.74 mm long | in the assemblies that use them | 68 |

The screws and inserts are objects of the [Fasteners workbench](https://github.com/shaise/FreeCAD_FastenersWB),
which the Addon Manager installs, made in the assembly that uses them: select one and change its type, diameter or
length in its properties, and its joint keeps it in its hole. Without the workbench the documents still open and show
them, but cannot change them. The controller board is designed in KiCad and is not part of this repository.

Each assembly sorts what it holds into `Printed_parts`, `Norm_parts` (screws and inserts), `COTS` (the other bought
parts) and `Assemblies` (sub-assemblies), and keeps its joints in `Joints`. Every joint joins two named coordinate
systems that the parts carry, never a face or an edge, so a part can be changed without breaking its joints; double
click a joint to edit it.

Every part shows in the colour of the real robot: mid grey printed PLA, charcoal TPU rollers, black screws,
brass inserts and spacers, a dark motor and a blue battery.

## Building it

The `.FCStd` files are generated; the scripts next to them are the source. To regenerate everything:

```
python3 code/rebuild.py
```

This needs FreeCAD 1.1 installed as the flatpak `org.freecad.FreeCAD`, with the Fasteners workbench. Each document
is also opened in the FreeCAD GUI briefly, to store its view, so that it opens showing the model, and to give its
joints and screws the view providers that the GUI's own commands would, so that a joint shows and can be edited.
With Xvfb installed (package `xvfb`) that happens on a private, invisible display and nothing appears on screen;
without it, or with `--show`, the windows open on the desktop. Single stages and single documents can be built on their own (`python3 code/rebuild.py --list`).

Each build tests what it makes. A part must recompute without a failed feature, its sketches must be fully
constrained, and each part changes a few of its named dimensions, recomputes and puts them back, to show that it
stays editable. An assembly must leave exactly the degrees of freedom it should, every joint's two coordinate systems
must coincide and the grounded part stay where it is, with no two parts overlapping and no part touching nothing; with `FCASM_SHAKE=1` it must also solve the same from a start with every moving
part pushed off its place. What each build found is written to `data/build/`.

## Layout

| Folder | What is in it |
|---|---|
| `model/` | The spreadsheet, the parts and the assemblies: each document next to the script that builds it. A module whose name starts with `_` describes a family of parts, such as the four wheel sides. `project.py` holds what the build needs to know about this model: the licence and what each part is made of, with its colour |
| `code/` | The build: `rebuild.py` drives FreeCAD, `build.py` runs one script inside it, `view.py` is the GUI stage, `bom.py` counts the bill of materials, `lib/` holds the helpers |
| `data/build/` | What each build found, one JSON file per document; `data/bom.json` is the bill of materials for tools |
| `BOM.md` | The bill of materials, generated by the build |
| `docs/` | How the parts are built |
